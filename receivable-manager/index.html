Why receivable management matters

Every unpaid invoice sitting with a customer is an account receivable — and without proper receivable management, it's easy to lose track of who owes what, and for how long.

The businesses that run out of cash rarely run out of sales — they run out of collected sales. Money sitting in a customer's account instead of yours is the single biggest silent killer of Nigerian SME cash flow, and it's usually invisible until it's a crisis.

Frequently asked questions

What is receivable management software?

Receivable management software tracks the money customers owe a business — also called accounts receivable — showing how overdue each balance is, sending reminders, and calculating how quickly the business gets paid (DSO), instead of tracking debts manually in a spreadsheet.

Do I need to use KoboDocs to invoice before I can track receivables?

No. Receivable Manager works standalone — add any outstanding balance manually, whether you invoice by bank transfer, WhatsApp, or paper. If you do use KoboDocs Business Suite to invoice, those invoices sync in automatically.

What is DSO and why does it matter?

Days Sales Outstanding measures the average number of days it takes to collect payment after a sale. A lower DSO means cash reaches your business faster — see the full explanation in our DSO guide for Nigerian SMEs.

How do overdue payment reminders work?

Each overdue client gets a pre-filled WhatsApp reminder you can send in one tap, with the outstanding balance already written in — no typing required.
