Before you sign

  • Sign in the presence of two witnesses, together, at the same time — not separately.
  • Your witnesses should not be beneficiaries under the will, or married to one — this can invalidate their gift.
  • Store the signed original somewhere safe, and tell your executor exactly where it is.
  • Review and update your will after major life events — marriage, divorce, a new child, or a significant change in what you own.
  • For a large or complex estate, or one likely to be contested, have a lawyer review this before you sign.

Frequently asked questions

Is a will generated online legally valid in Nigeria?
A will's validity depends on it being properly signed and witnessed according to the Wills Act/Law applicable in your state — the document itself must still be executed correctly to be valid, regardless of how it was drafted.
