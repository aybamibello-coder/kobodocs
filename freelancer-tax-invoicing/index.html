Why this matters now

A growing number of Nigerian designers, developers, writers and consultants earn mainly from clients abroad. Until this year, that income mostly flew under the radar. That changed with the Nigeria Tax Act 2025.

Under the new law, Nigerian tax residents are taxed on worldwide income — including what lands in your Payoneer, Wise, or domiciliary account from a US or UK client. Nigerian clients can withhold tax on your behalf; foreign clients can't, so it's on you to register, keep records, and self-assess.

Frequently asked questions

Do freelancers in Nigeria pay tax on income from foreign clients?

Yes, if you're a Nigerian tax resident. Under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 (effective January 2026), residents are taxed on worldwide income — including USD, GBP or EUR earned from clients abroad, even paid into Payoneer, Wise, or a domiciliary account. Non-residents are generally not taxed on foreign income.

What happens if I don't register or file?

Failing to register brings a ₦50,000 fine the first month, then ₦25,000 for every month after. Failing to file returns brings ₦100,000 the first month, then ₦50,000 for every month after. False declarations can bring fines up to ₦1,000,000 or imprisonment.

How do I invoice a foreign client properly?

Bill in the client's own currency (usually USD or GBP), state the currency clearly, and keep a copy — invoices are part of the record FIRS expects you to retain. Our free invoice generator supports NGN, USD, GBP and EUR directly. See the full invoicing guide for what else to include.

What can I deduct as a freelancer?

Commonly cited deductible expenses include internet and data costs, software subscriptions, up to 20% of annual rent (capped at ₦500,000), pension contributions (8%), NHF (2.5% where applicable), and life insurance premiums up to ₦100,000. Confirm what applies to your situation with an accountant.
