How to Calculate PAYE, Pension & NHF on a Nigerian Payslip (2026 Guide)

July 2026 · 11 min read

If you've ever stared at a Nigerian payslip trying to figure out why gross pay and take-home pay are so far apart, you're not alone. Between PAYE tax, pension, and NHF, there are three separate deductions stacking on top of each other — each calculated slightly differently. This guide breaks down exactly how each one works, using the current bands under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 (effective 1 January 2026), with a full worked example.

Key takeaways
  • The first ₦800,000 of annual taxable income is tax-free under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 (effective 1 January 2026).
  • Pension is 8% of basic + housing + transport (employee share) — not your full gross pay.
  • NHF is 2.5% of basic salary only, not gross pay.
  • PAYE is calculated on taxable income — gross pay minus pension, NHF, and rent relief — not on gross pay directly.
  • Rent relief is 20% of annual rent, capped at ₦500,000 per year.

What a compliant Nigerian payslip should show

  • Employee and employer names, and the pay period it covers.
  • Gross pay breakdown — basic salary, housing allowance, transport allowance, and any other allowances, shown separately rather than as one lump figure.
  • Each deduction shown individually — pension, NHF, and PAYE tax, not netted off silently.
  • Net pay — what actually lands in the employee's account.

Showing each deduction separately isn't just good practice — it's what makes a payslip useful as supporting evidence for a loan application, a visa application, or a rental agreement, all of which typically want to see the breakdown, not just a final number.

Pension: 8% of basic, housing, and transport

Under the Pension Reform Act, an employee typically contributes 8% of basic salary, housing allowance, and transport allowance combined (not the full gross pay) toward their retirement savings account, with the employer required to contribute at least 10% on top of that. Only the employee's 8% is deducted from the payslip itself — the employer's 10% is paid separately and doesn't reduce take-home pay.

NHF: 2.5% of basic salary

The National Housing Fund deduction is 2.5% of basic salary only — not the full gross pay, and not including housing or transport allowances. It's a smaller deduction than pension for most salary structures, but it's still a mandatory one for qualifying employees, and it should appear on the payslip as its own line rather than folded into a general "deductions" total.

PAYE: the progressive tax bands (2026)

Personal Income Tax under PAYE is calculated on your taxable income — gross pay, minus pension, minus NHF, minus any rent relief you qualify for — using progressive bands, meaning different portions of your income are taxed at different rates, not your whole salary at one flat rate. Under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025, effective from 1 January 2026, the annual bands are:

  • ₦0 – ₦800,000: 0%
  • Next ₦2,200,000 (up to ₦3,000,000): 15%
  • Next ₦9,000,000 (up to ₦12,000,000): 18%
  • Next ₦13,000,000 (up to ₦25,000,000): 21%
  • Next ₦25,000,000 (up to ₦50,000,000): 23%
  • Above ₦50,000,000: 25%

The first ₦800,000 of taxable annual income is completely tax-free — this is a significant change from the old bands and means many lower-income earners now pay little to no PAYE at all.

Rent relief: up to ₦500,000 a year

If you pay rent, you can deduct 20% of your annual rent from your taxable income before PAYE is calculated — capped at ₦500,000 per year, whichever of the two is lower. For someone paying ₦2,000,000 a year in rent, 20% is ₦400,000 — under the cap, so the full ₦400,000 applies. For someone paying ₦3,000,000 a year, 20% is ₦600,000 — but the relief is still capped at ₦500,000, the maximum allowed.

A full worked example

Let's say a Lagos-based employee has:

  • Basic salary: ₦150,000/month (₦1,800,000/year)
  • Housing allowance: ₦50,000/month (₦600,000/year)
  • Transport allowance: ₦30,000/month (₦360,000/year)
  • Annual rent paid: ₦800,000

Step 1 — Gross annual pay: ₦1,800,000 + ₦600,000 + ₦360,000 = ₦2,760,000

Step 2 — Pension (8% of basic + housing + transport): 8% × ₦2,760,000 = ₦220,800

Step 3 — NHF (2.5% of basic only): 2.5% × ₦1,800,000 = ₦45,000

Step 4 — Rent relief: 20% of ₦800,000 = ₦160,000 (under the ₦500,000 cap, so the full amount applies)

Step 5 — Taxable income: ₦2,760,000 − ₦220,800 − ₦45,000 − ₦160,000 = ₦2,334,200

Step 6 — PAYE, applied band by band:

  • First ₦800,000 at 0% = ₦0
  • Remaining ₦1,534,200 at 15% = ₦230,130

Total annual PAYE: ₦230,130 — or about ₦19,178 per month.

Net monthly pay works out to: ₦230,000 (gross monthly) − ₦18,400 (pension) − ₦3,750 (NHF) − ₦19,178 (PAYE) ≈ ₦188,672 take-home per month, from a gross of ₦230,000.

Why this trips people up

The two most common mistakes: applying PAYE to gross pay instead of taxable income (forgetting to subtract pension, NHF, and rent relief first), and applying pension to full gross pay instead of just basic + housing + transport. Both mistakes overstate deductions and understate take-home pay — which matters not just for payroll accuracy but for things like loan affordability calculations, which often use net pay as a key input.

Why a proper payslip matters beyond payday

A payslip isn't just a payday formality — it's frequently required for loan applications, visa applications (particularly for relocation — see our relocation planning tools), rental agreements, and mortgage applications. Lenders and embassies want to see the full breakdown — gross, each deduction, and net — not just a final take-home figure, since it demonstrates both income level and that deductions are being handled properly.

Frequently asked questions

Is pension calculated on gross pay or just basic salary?
Neither exactly — it's calculated on basic salary plus housing and transport allowances combined (8% employee contribution), not your full gross pay if you have other allowances beyond those three, and not basic salary alone.

Do I still pay PAYE if my salary is low?
Under the current bands, the first ₦800,000 of annual taxable income is tax-free, so many lower earners — especially after pension, NHF, and rent relief are subtracted — may owe little or no PAYE at all.

Does rent relief apply automatically?
It applies if you claim it and can show you pay rent — 20% of your annual rent, capped at ₦500,000 per year. It's not deducted automatically without being accounted for in the payroll calculation.

Is NHF compulsory for every employee?
NHF applies to qualifying employees under the National Housing Fund Act, generally those earning above a minimum threshold — check with your employer's payroll or HR team on whether it applies to your specific role and salary level.

Do allowances beyond housing and transport count toward pension?
No — the standard 8% employee pension contribution is calculated on basic salary, housing, and transport only. Other allowances (such as a lunch or utility allowance) are typically excluded from that base, though gross pay for tax purposes still includes them.
