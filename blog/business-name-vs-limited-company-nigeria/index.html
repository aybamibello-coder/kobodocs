Business Name vs Limited Company in Nigeria: Which Should You Register?

August 2026 · 6 min read

Almost every new business owner in Nigeria hits this decision early: register a Business Name, or go straight to a Limited Company (Ltd)? Both are valid ways to formalize a business with CAC, and neither is universally "better" — they trade off differently on cost, liability, and how the business is allowed to grow.

Key takeaways
  • A Business Name is cheaper and faster to register, with no legal separation between owner and business.
  • A Limited Company protects personal assets from business debts, but costs more and needs a lawyer for part of the process.
  • Many businesses start as a Business Name and incorporate later once they've grown — it's not a permanent choice.
  • Some clients, grants, and government contracts require Limited Company status specifically.

What actually differs between them

Business NameLimited Company
Legal separationNone — owner is personally liable for business debtsSeparate legal entity — personal assets generally protected
Registration cost & speedLower cost, typically fasterHigher cost, more documentation
Ownership structureSole proprietor or simple partnershipShareholders, directors, formal governance
Perceived credibilityFine for most small clientsOften preferred for larger contracts, grants, foreign clients
Ongoing obligationsSimpler, fewer filingsAnnual Returns, more formal record-keeping

Why liability protection matters more than it seems

The legal separation a Limited Company provides isn't just paperwork — it's about what happens if the business runs into serious trouble. If a Business Name accumulates debt it can't pay, that debt is treated as the owner's personal debt. A Limited Company's debts, in most circumstances, stay with the company rather than reaching into the owner's personal assets. For a business taking on real financial risk — inventory financing, equipment loans, larger client contracts — this distinction is worth the extra registration effort.

When a Business Name is the right call

For a freelancer, a solo service provider, or a small operation with low financial risk and mostly individual or small-business clients, a Business Name is often genuinely sufficient. It's faster to set up, cheaper, and easier to maintain — and there's no rule saying a business has to start at maximum formality. Plenty of businesses run successfully as a Business Name for years before ever needing to incorporate.

You can switch later. Starting as a Business Name doesn't lock you out of incorporating as a Limited Company down the line — it's a common path as a business grows, takes on bigger contracts, or needs the credibility and liability protection incorporation provides. It's a fresh registration process rather than an automatic upgrade, but it's a well-worn path, not an unusual one.

When it's worth going straight to a Limited Company

Some situations make the extra cost and complexity worth it from day one: applying for grants or funds that specifically require incorporated status, working with larger corporate or government clients who only contract with Limited Companies, bringing on co-founders or investors who need formal shareholding, or taking on financial risk significant enough that personal liability protection matters immediately rather than eventually.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a lawyer to register a Business Name?
No — a Business Name registration is straightforward enough that many people handle it themselves directly through CAC's portal, without needing legal assistance for the registration itself.

Is a Limited Company always required for a lawyer step?
Yes, at least for one part — CAC requires a Limited Company's Memorandum and Articles of Association to be prepared by a licensed legal practitioner, which is not the case for a Business Name.

Does either option affect my tax obligations differently?
Tax treatment can differ by business structure, and it's worth confirming the specifics for your situation with an accountant rather than assuming either structure is automatically simpler on the tax side.
