Company Income Tax Exemption for Small Businesses: Do You Qualify?

August 2026 · 6 min read

A lot of small business owners are still budgeting for Company Income Tax they may no longer owe. Nigeria's 2026 tax reform significantly raised the turnover threshold under which a company pays 0% Company Income Tax — and a surprising number of eligible small businesses either don't know they qualify, or aren't sure how to confirm it.

Key takeaways
  • Under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025, companies with annual turnover up to ₦100 million qualify as a "small company" and pay 0% Company Income Tax.
  • This is a significant increase from the previous, much lower small-company threshold.
  • The exemption applies to turnover, not profit — a company can have modest profit margins and still need to check where its total turnover actually sits.
  • Qualifying as exempt doesn't remove your filing obligation — you still need to file returns, just without CIT liability if you're under the threshold.
  • Tax authorities are actively flagging businesses whose bank-tracked turnover exceeds ₦100 million but who continue claiming the 0% rate — accurate self-assessment matters.

The headline change

Under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025, a "small company" — one with annual turnover of ₦100 million or less — pays 0% Company Income Tax. This is a substantial jump from the earlier threshold, meaning a meaningful number of small and growing Nigerian businesses that previously owed CIT may now fall entirely outside it.

Turnover, not profit

This is where confusion often sets in: the exemption threshold is based on turnover (total revenue), not net profit. A business with thin margins but high sales volume needs to check its actual turnover figure against the ₦100 million line, regardless of how much profit is left after expenses. Don't assume a business is automatically "small" just because profits feel modest — the qualifying test is about total revenue.

What else comes with small-company status

Qualifying as a small company under the current rules generally means exemption from Company Income Tax, Capital Gains Tax, and the Development Levy. It's worth understanding this as a package of related reliefs tied to the same turnover threshold, rather than a single isolated tax break.

Exemption doesn't mean no filing

This is a common and costly misunderstanding: qualifying for 0% CIT doesn't mean you're excused from filing returns. Annual filing obligations still apply — you're filing to demonstrate you qualify for the 0% rate, not skipping the process entirely. Missing a filing deadline can trigger real penalties regardless of your actual tax liability being zero.

Why accuracy matters here

Tax authorities have increasingly automated ways of cross-checking claimed turnover against actual bank-tracked activity, and businesses whose real turnover exceeds ₦100 million while still claiming small-company status are being flagged. If your business is genuinely close to the threshold — growing quickly, or with turnover that fluctuates year to year — it's worth checking your actual numbers each year rather than assuming last year's status still applies.

What to do if you're close to the threshold

  • Calculate actual annual turnover carefully, not an estimate — this is the number that determines everything else.
  • If you're near ₦100 million and growing, plan ahead for the year you'll likely cross it, rather than being caught off guard by a change in liability.
  • Keep filing on time regardless of your exemption status — the filing obligation and the tax liability are separate things.
  • If your turnover is genuinely borderline or complex to calculate (multiple revenue streams, part-year trading), get a professional to confirm the number rather than estimating.

Frequently asked questions

What is the small company turnover threshold for Company Income Tax exemption in Nigeria?
Under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025, companies with annual turnover of ₦100 million or less qualify as a small company and pay 0% Company Income Tax.

Is the CIT exemption based on turnover or profit?
Turnover (total revenue), not net profit. A business should check its actual total revenue against the ₦100 million threshold regardless of its profit margins.

If my company is exempt from Company Income Tax, do I still need to file returns?
Yes — exemption from CIT doesn't remove the filing obligation. You still need to file annual returns to demonstrate you qualify for the 0% rate; missing the filing deadline can trigger penalties even with zero tax liability.

What other taxes are small companies exempt from besides CIT?
Small companies under the ₦100 million threshold are generally also exempt from Capital Gains Tax and the Development Levy, as part of the same relief package.

What happens if my business's turnover exceeds ₦100 million but I keep claiming the small-company rate?
Tax authorities have increasingly automated ways of cross-checking claimed turnover against actual bank activity, and businesses exceeding the threshold while still claiming exemption are being flagged — it's worth confirming your actual turnover each year rather than assuming your status hasn't changed.
