Nigerian Business Compliance Deadlines: The Full Calendar

August 2026 · 6 min read

Between VAT, PAYE, pension, CAC filings, and annual tax returns, a registered Nigerian business is juggling several recurring deadlines at once — some monthly, some annual, each with its own penalty structure if missed. None of them are individually complicated. The risk is usually forgetting one exists, not misunderstanding it once you know about it.

Key takeaways
  • VAT (21st) and PAYE (10th) are monthly deadlines — easy to lose track of because they recur so often.
  • Pension remittance (7th) is monthly too, and often the first one people forget since it's paid to a PFA, not FIRS directly.
  • CAC Annual Return and Company Income Tax filing are once-a-year deadlines with much steeper consequences for being forgotten entirely.
  • Since 2026, several of these carry real financial penalties, not just late notices — 40% on under-deducted PAYE, 10% on late pension.

Every one of these is small enough to handle in isolation — the difficulty is entirely about remembering they're recurring, not about the filing itself being hard.

Why the monthly ones catch people out

Annual deadlines get remembered because they only come around once — there's a natural sense of "tax season" attached to them. Monthly deadlines don't get that same mental flag. Pension, PAYE, and VAT all fall within roughly two weeks of each other every single month, and it's easy for the third or fourth month of a new business to slip by with one of them missed simply because nothing marked the date.

We've covered what a correct payroll calculation actually requires and the 2026 PAYE bands in detail elsewhere — this is about the calendar side of the same problem: getting the number right doesn't help if the remittance itself is late.

Why the annual ones matter even though they're rare

CAC Annual Return and Company Income Tax filing only happen once a year, which makes them easy to genuinely forget rather than just run late on. Missing an Annual Return for multiple years in a row is one of the more common reasons a company ends up administratively struck off CAC's register without the owner realizing it happened — often discovered only when the business tries to do something that requires an active CAC status, like opening a new bank account or applying for a loan.

What's changed since 2026. Several of these deadlines now carry real financial penalties rather than just a late notice. Under-deducted PAYE carries a penalty of up to 40%, late pension remittance carries a 10% surcharge, and CAC has moved to actively enforcing disclosure and filing requirements it previously treated more loosely. See our guides on cash flow and the CAC letterhead checker for related compliance changes from the same reforms.

A simple way to stop relying on memory

The businesses that consistently hit these deadlines rarely do it by remembering better — they do it by having something external track the dates for them, the same way a calendar reminder works better than trying to hold a friend's birthday in your head. For a small business, that doesn't need to be complicated: a running list of obligations with due dates, checked once a week, catches almost everything before it becomes urgent.

Frequently asked questions

What happens if I miss the VAT filing deadline?
Late VAT filing typically attracts penalties and interest under FIRS rules — the specific amounts can change, so it's worth confirming current figures directly with FIRS or an accountant if you're already past a deadline.

Do sole proprietorships (Business Names) have the same obligations as registered companies?
Some obligations differ by business structure — a Business Name doesn't file a CAC Annual Return the same way a limited company does, for example. It's worth confirming which specific obligations apply to your registration type rather than assuming the full list applies uniformly.

Can these deadlines be tracked alongside invoicing and other business records?
There's no requirement that they must be separate — keeping obligation deadlines, document renewals, and financial records in one place tends to reduce the number of things a business owner has to remember to check.
