Nigeria's 2026 PAYE Tax Bands Explained, With a Worked Example

August 2026 · 8 min read

The Nigeria Tax Act, effective from January 2026, changed how PAYE is calculated more significantly than any reform in years — a much higher tax-free threshold, a new set of progressive bands, and rent relief replacing the old Consolidated Relief Allowance (CRA). If your last mental model of "how much tax comes out of my salary" is from before 2026, it's worth updating.

Key takeaways
  • The first ₦800,000 of annual taxable income is tax-free — a significant increase from the old system's threshold.
  • Tax is progressive across six bands, from 0% up to 25% — you only pay the higher rate on the portion of income within each band, not your entire income.
  • The old Consolidated Relief Allowance (CRA) is gone, replaced by a rent relief: 20% of your annual rent, capped at ₦500,000.
  • Pension (8% of basic + housing + transport) and NHF (2.5% of basic) remain deductible before tax is calculated.
  • Because the rules changed this substantially, it's worth recalculating your actual take-home rather than assuming your old numbers still apply.

The 2026 tax bands

PAYE is calculated on annual taxable income (after deductions) using six progressive bands:

Annual taxable incomeRate
First ₦800,0000%
Next ₦2,200,000 (₦800,001 – ₦3,000,000)15%
Next ₦9,000,000 (₦3,000,001 – ₦12,000,000)18%
Next ₦13,000,000 (₦12,000,001 – ₦25,000,000)21%
Next ₦25,000,000 (₦25,000,001 – ₦50,000,000)23%
Above ₦50,000,00025%

These are marginal rates — each band's rate only applies to the portion of income that falls within it, not your whole income. Someone with ₦4,000,000 in taxable income isn't taxed at 18% across the board; the first ₦800,000 is tax-free, the next ₦2,200,000 is taxed at 15%, and only the remaining ₦1,000,000 is taxed at 18%.

What's deductible before tax is calculated

  • Pension contribution — 8% of (basic + housing + transport), fully deductible.
  • National Housing Fund (NHF) — 2.5% of basic salary, where applicable.
  • Rent relief — 20% of your annual rent, capped at ₦500,000. This replaces the old CRA entirely.

Your gross annual income, minus these deductions, gives your taxable income — that's the figure the bands above are actually applied to, not your raw salary.

What changed from the old system

The old Consolidated Relief Allowance is gone. Under the previous system, CRA gave a flat relief regardless of your actual housing costs. The new rent relief ties the deduction to what you actually pay in rent (20%, capped at ₦500,000) — which can be more or less generous than the old CRA depending on your specific numbers.

Two changes matter most: the tax-free threshold moved up substantially (meaning lower earners now pay significantly less or nothing at all), and CRA was replaced by rent relief tied to actual rent paid rather than a flat allowance. For renters with high rent relative to income, this can be a meaningful shift either way — it's worth recalculating rather than assuming the old and new systems land in the same place for your specific salary.

A worked example

Take a monthly gross salary of ₦500,000 (₦6,000,000 annually), split as ₦250,000 basic, ₦100,000 housing, ₦100,000 transport, and ₦50,000 other allowances, with annual rent of ₦1,800,000:

  • Pension: 8% × (₦250,000 + ₦100,000 + ₦100,000) × 12 = ₦432,000
  • NHF: 2.5% × ₦250,000 × 12 = ₦75,000
  • Rent relief: 20% × ₦1,800,000 = ₦360,000 (under the ₦500,000 cap, so the full amount applies)
  • Taxable income: ₦6,000,000 − ₦432,000 − ₦75,000 − ₦360,000 = ₦5,133,000
  • Tax: ₦0 (first ₦800,000) + 15% × ₦2,200,000 (₦330,000) + 18% × ₦2,133,000 (₦383,940) = ₦713,940 annually, or roughly ₦59,495 monthly

On a ₦500,000 monthly gross, that works out to a take-home of roughly ₦398,255 after pension, NHF, and PAYE — the exact figure depends on your specific salary structure and rent.

Frequently asked questions

Do I automatically get rent relief, or do I need to claim it?
You generally need to provide evidence of rent payment to your employer for it to be applied in payroll — it isn't necessarily automatic without documentation.

Is NHF mandatory for everyone?
This depends on sector and employment type — confirm with your employer or payroll provider whether it applies to your specific situation, as treatment can differ between public and private sector employment.

What if my rent is more than ₦2,500,000 a year?
The rent relief is capped at ₦500,000 regardless of how high your actual rent is — 20% of annual rent up to that ceiling, and no higher, no matter your rent amount.

Do these bands apply to freelancers and self-employed income too?
Personal income tax principles broadly apply, but self-employed and freelance income can involve different filing and deduction considerations than salaried PAYE — it's worth confirming your specific obligations rather than assuming identical treatment.

Will these bands change again soon?
Tax law is subject to further review and amendment over time. It's worth checking for updates periodically rather than assuming today's bands are permanent.
