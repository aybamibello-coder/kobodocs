Receivable Management Services vs. Accounts Receivable Software: What Nigerian Businesses Actually Need

August 2026 · 6 min read

Once a business has enough customers on credit terms to lose track of who owes what, two paths tend to come up: hire receivable management services to chase the money on your behalf, or use accounts receivable software to track and chase it yourself. They solve the same underlying problem — cash sitting uncollected in unpaid invoices — but they're built for different situations, and picking the wrong one either overspends on a problem software could have solved, or leaves a business trying to manage something that's genuinely outgrown a spreadsheet.

Key takeaways
  • Receivable management services outsource the chasing to a third party, usually for a flat fee or a cut of what's recovered.
  • Accounts receivable software keeps the business in control — it shows what's owed and helps you chase it, without handing over the client relationship.
  • For most small and medium businesses, software solves the actual problem (visibility and consistent follow-up) at a fraction of the cost of a service.
  • The main reason businesses skip both and stay on spreadsheets is that no one has time to update it — which is exactly what causes the late payments in the first place.

What receivable management services actually do

Outsourced receivable management services — sometimes structured as collections agencies, sometimes as fractional finance or credit-control providers — take over the process of chasing customers for payment. A business hands over its outstanding balances, and the service handles follow-up calls, reminder letters, and negotiation, usually charging either a flat monthly fee or a percentage of whatever they successfully recover.

This can make sense for businesses with a large, unwieldy book of receivables, receivables that are seriously overdue and need a firmer hand than an internal team is set up to apply, or simply no internal capacity to do collections work at all. The tradeoff is cost (percentage-based fees in particular can be steep on larger balances) and a layer of separation between the business and its own customers during what's often already a sensitive conversation.

What accounts receivable software actually does

Accounts receivable software takes a different approach: instead of outsourcing the work, it makes doing the work yourself fast enough that it actually gets done. At its core, this means tracking every outstanding balance, showing how overdue each one is (commonly bucketed as current, 1–30 days, 31–60 days, 61+ days overdue), and making it fast to send a reminder — rather than relying on someone remembering to check a spreadsheet and manually typing out a WhatsApp message.

The business stays in direct contact with its own customers throughout, which matters for ongoing relationships, and the cost is typically a small fixed monthly fee rather than a cut of every collection.

Most late payment isn't a collections problem — it's a visibility problem. The majority of overdue invoices aren't stuck because a customer is refusing to pay; they're stuck because no one flagged them as overdue and sent a reminder at the right moment. That's exactly what accounts receivable software fixes, without the cost or relationship friction of outsourcing.

Days Sales Outstanding (DSO): the number that tells you which one you need

Days Sales Outstanding measures the average number of days it takes a business to collect payment after a sale — a lower number means cash reaches the business faster. Tracking DSO over time is one of the clearest signals for which path makes sense: if DSO climbs steadily despite consistent follow-up, that points toward needing more collections firepower (a service). If DSO is high mainly because follow-up simply isn't happening consistently, software that makes reminders effortless usually closes most of the gap on its own.

Frequently asked questions

Can a business use both — software first, then a service for the worst cases?
Yes, and this is a common pattern — use software to keep on top of receivables day to day, and reserve an outsourced service for balances that have gone seriously overdue despite consistent internal follow-up.

Do I need separate invoicing software to use accounts receivable software?
Not necessarily — some accounts receivable software works standalone, letting you add any outstanding balance manually regardless of how the original invoice was sent (bank transfer, WhatsApp, or paper), while also syncing automatically with invoicing software if you use it.

How much does accounts receivable software typically cost compared to a service?
Software is usually priced as a small fixed monthly fee, while services often charge a percentage of what they recover — meaning the cost of a service scales with your receivables book in a way software's fixed cost doesn't.
