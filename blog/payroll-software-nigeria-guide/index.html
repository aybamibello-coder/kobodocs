Running Payroll in Nigeria: What Every Small Business Actually Needs to Get Right

August 2026 · 7 min read

A lot of small Nigerian businesses run payroll the same way for years: an Excel sheet, a rough sense of what to deduct, and a payslip typed up fresh each month. It works, mostly — until a tax band changes, an employee asks why their deduction looks different from a colleague's, or a mistake goes unnoticed for months. The 2026 Nigeria Tax Act raised the cost of getting this wrong, which makes it a good time to look at what "correct" payroll actually requires.

Key takeaways
  • Three deductions have to be right every month: PAYE tax, pension (8% employee / 10% employer), and NHF (2.5% of basic, where applicable).
  • Since 2026, under-deducted PAYE carries a penalty of up to 40%, and late pension remittance carries a 10% surcharge — real money, not just a correction notice.
  • PAYE is calculated on taxable income after pension and NHF are deducted — not on gross salary directly.
  • A small team doesn't need enterprise HR software — just something that calculates these three things correctly and produces a real payslip.

The three deductions that have to be right

PAYE (Pay As You Earn) tax. Calculated on annual taxable income using the 2026 bands (0% up to ₦800,000, then progressively up to 25% — see our full PAYE guide for the complete table and a worked example). Taxable income isn't gross salary — pension, NHF, and rent relief are deducted first.

Pension. 8% of the employee's basic + housing + transport allowance, deducted from their pay, plus a matching 10% employer contribution that the business pays on top (not deducted from the employee). Both go to the employee's Pension Fund Administrator.

National Housing Fund (NHF). 2.5% of basic salary, where the employee is enrolled. Whether this applies can depend on sector and employment type — worth confirming for your specific business rather than assuming either way.

Why the stakes went up in 2026

The 2026 Nigeria Tax Act introduced real financial penalties for payroll errors. Under-deducted PAYE now carries a penalty of up to 40% of the shortfall, and late pension remittance carries a 10% surcharge. Previously, a payroll mistake was often just quietly corrected the following month — that's no longer a safe assumption.

This changes the calculus for small businesses that have been running payroll informally. A spreadsheet error that used to cost nothing but embarrassment can now cost real money — and the kind of small arithmetic mistakes that creep into manual spreadsheets (an outdated tax band, a missed allowance in the pensionable calculation) are exactly what these penalties target.

What a correct payslip actually shows

A proper payslip breaks down gross pay into its components (basic, housing, transport, other allowances), shows each deduction separately (pension, NHF, PAYE), and arrives at a net pay figure that the employee can verify themselves. This transparency matters beyond compliance — employees who can see exactly how their net pay was calculated ask fewer questions and trust the process more than those handed a single number with no breakdown.

Running payroll for a small team, practically

For a business with five, ten, or twenty employees, the actual workflow each month is simple in principle: confirm each employee's current salary structure, run the calculation, generate payslips, and process the payments and statutory remittances. Where it goes wrong is usually in the calculation step — using last year's tax bands, forgetting to update pensionable pay when someone gets a raise, or miscounting which allowances count toward NHF.

This is exactly the kind of repetitive, rules-based calculation that's worth automating rather than redoing by hand every month. Add each employee's salary structure once; the calculation itself shouldn't need to be re-derived from scratch every payroll run.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need full HR software just to run payroll for a small team?
No — a small business doesn't need recruitment tracking, leave management, and performance reviews just to calculate deductions correctly. A focused payroll tool that handles PAYE, pension, and NHF calculation and produces real payslips covers what actually matters for compliance.

Is pension mandatory for a small business?
Pension obligations can depend on business size and employee count under Nigerian pension law — worth confirming your specific obligations rather than assuming a blanket rule either way.

What if an employee's salary changes mid-year?
The calculation should simply use whatever their current salary structure is for that pay period — there's no need to recalculate prior months when a salary changes going forward.

Can payroll and invoicing live in the same place?
There's no requirement that they must, but keeping payroll alongside the rest of a business's financial documents (invoices, receipts, quotations) in one place tends to reduce the number of separate logins and spreadsheets a business owner has to juggle.
