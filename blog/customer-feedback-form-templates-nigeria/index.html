Customer Feedback Form Templates for Nigerian Businesses: What to Actually Ask

September 2026 · 5 min read

Most customer feedback forms fail for one of two reasons: they ask too many questions and nobody finishes them, or they ask vague questions ("How was your experience?") that produce answers too generic to act on. A good feedback form is short, specific, and asks about things you can actually change.

Key takeaways
  • Keep a customer feedback form to 3-5 questions — completion rate drops sharply past that.
  • Mix one rating-scale question (fast to answer) with one open-text question (captures detail a scale can't).
  • Ask about specific moments (checkout, delivery time, staff interaction) rather than "overall experience."
  • Share the form immediately after the interaction — a receipt QR code or WhatsApp follow-up gets far higher response rates than an email sent days later.

What a short, effective feedback form looks like

A feedback form that gets completed and produces useful answers usually has this shape:

  • One overall rating — a 1-5 star rating or a simple satisfied/neutral/dissatisfied choice.
  • One specific rating — about the part of the experience you're most trying to improve (wait time, product quality, staff friendliness).
  • One open-text question — "What's one thing we could do better?" gives you detail the ratings alone won't.
  • Optional contact field — only if you want to follow up on negative feedback directly, and make it clearly optional.

Templates by business type

Retail / shop feedback

Rate your shopping experience → Rate product availability (did we have what you were looking for?) → What's one thing we could improve? → (Optional) Phone number if you'd like us to follow up.

Restaurant / food service feedback

Rate your meal → Rate the wait time → How was the service from our staff? → What's one thing we could improve?

Service business feedback (salon, repair, professional services)

Rate the work done → Was the timeline what you expected? → Would you use us again? → What's one thing we could improve?

Timing matters more than most people think. A feedback form linked on the receipt (via QR code) or sent by WhatsApp within an hour of the visit gets a meaningfully higher response rate than one emailed a day or two later — by then, the specific details of the experience have already faded.

What to do with the responses

Feedback that just sits in a spreadsheet doesn't improve anything. Set a simple rule: review responses weekly, flag anything scored 2 stars or below for a direct follow-up, and track your average rating month to month so you can tell if a change you made actually helped.

Frequently asked questions

Should feedback forms be anonymous?
Making the contact field optional (rather than fully anonymous) tends to work best — most customers will still leave honest feedback, and you keep the option to follow up on serious complaints.

How do I get customers to actually fill it out?
A QR code on the receipt or a WhatsApp message sent immediately after the visit works far better than relying on customers to remember later. Keeping it to under a minute to complete also matters more than any incentive.

Is a star rating enough, or do I need open-text too?
Use both — a star rating alone tells you something is wrong without telling you what, while open-text alone is slower to complete and harder to track over time.
