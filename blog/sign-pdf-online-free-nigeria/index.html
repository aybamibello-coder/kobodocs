How to Sign a PDF Online for Free in Nigeria (Step-by-Step)

September 2026 · 6 min read

There are two completely different things people mean by "sign a PDF online," and they need different tools. If you just need to add your own signature to a single document — a job offer, a school form, a tenancy agreement someone emailed you — you can do that free, in your browser, in under two minutes. If you need to send a document to someone else and know when they've signed it, that's a different job, and it needs a proper e-signature tool, not just a PDF editor. This guide covers both.

Key takeaways
  • Signing a PDF you already have — just your own signature, on your own document — is genuinely free and doesn't need an account with most tools, KoboDocs included.
  • Sending a document to someone else for signature is a different task: you need delivery, a way for them to sign without downloading anything, and proof of who signed and when.
  • An e-signature is legally valid in Nigeria under the Evidence Act and CAMA — see our full legal breakdown if that's the question you actually came here with.
  • KoboDocs e-Signature starts at ₦500 per document with no monthly commitment, for when you're the one sending, not just signing.

Method 1: Signing a PDF you already have (free, your own document)

Use this when someone has sent you a PDF and you just need to add your signature and send it back.

  1. Open the PDF in KoboDocs e-Signature. Go to kobodocs.com.ng/esign and upload the file — no account needed for this step.
  2. Draw or type your signature. Use your finger or mouse to draw it, or type your name in a signature-style font — either is accepted.
  3. Place it on the document. Drag your signature to the right spot — usually the signature line or the bottom of the last page.
  4. Download the signed PDF. You get back a completed PDF with your signature embedded, ready to email or send on WhatsApp.
This method works the same way in most free PDF tools, not just KoboDocs — Adobe Acrobat's free web tool and Smallpdf both offer a similar single-document "sign and download" flow. The difference matters once you need to track whether someone else has signed, which is Method 2 below.

Method 2: Sending a document out for someone else to sign

Use this when you're the one who needs a signature from someone else — a client accepting a quotation, a new hire signing an offer letter, a landlord and tenant both signing a tenancy agreement.

  1. Upload the document to KoboDocs e-Signature and add the signer's name and phone number or email.
  2. Mark where they need to sign. Place a signature field on the document for each person who needs to sign.
  3. Send it. The signer gets a link — they don't need a KoboDocs account, a password, or to download anything. They open the link, sign, and it's done.
  4. Track status in real time. You'll see when it's been opened, and when it's been signed, without having to ask.
  5. Get the signed copy with an audit trail. The final PDF includes a record of who signed, when, and from what device — useful if the document is ever disputed.

Which method do you actually need?

  • Signing your own copy of something someone sent you? Method 1 — free, no account, one document.
  • Sending a contract, offer letter, or agreement to someone else? Method 2 — you need delivery and tracking, not just a signature tool.
  • Need multiple people to sign the same document? Method 2 handles sequential or parallel signers on one document.
  • Worried whether it'll hold up if challenged later? See the legal validity guide — short answer: yes, under the Evidence Act and CAMA, provided there's a proper audit trail.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to install anything to sign a PDF online?
No — both methods above work entirely in your browser. Nothing to install, and the person you send a document to doesn't need to install anything either.

Is a signature added this way legally binding in Nigeria?
Yes, provided the platform keeps an audit trail (who signed, when, from where). See the full legal guide for the specifics under the Evidence Act and CAMA.

Can I sign a PDF on my phone?
Yes — both methods work on mobile browsers; you draw your signature with your finger instead of a mouse.

What if I need more than one person to sign the same document?
Use Method 2 — add each signer and where they need to sign, and KoboDocs tracks each signature as it comes in.
