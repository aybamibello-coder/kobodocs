Take-Home Pay in Nigeria 2026: How Much You Actually Keep

August 2026 · 8 min read

If your salary "changed" this year without you getting a raise, it's not your imagination — Nigeria's tax bands changed on 1 January 2026, and they genuinely shifted how much lands in your account every month. Whether that's good or bad news depends entirely on how much you earn.

Here's what actually changed, and how to work out your real take-home pay under the new rules.

Key takeaways
  • The first ₦800,000 of annual taxable income is now completely tax-free — up sharply from the old threshold.
  • Above that, tax is progressive: 15% up to ₦3,000,000, 18% up to ₦12,000,000, 21% up to ₦25,000,000, 23% up to ₦50,000,000, and 25% above that.
  • The old Consolidated Relief Allowance (CRA) is gone, replaced by a rent relief of 20% of annual rent paid, capped at ₦500,000.
  • Pension (8% of basic + housing + transport) still applies as before.
  • NHF (2.5%) is now voluntary for private-sector employees, though still mandatory in the public sector.

The headline change: ₦800,000 is now tax-free

Under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025, effective from 1 January 2026, the first ₦800,000 of your annual taxable income — roughly ₦66,667 a month — attracts zero PAYE. That's a meaningful jump from the old system, and it means lower earners, in particular, keep noticeably more of their salary than they did in 2025.

The new tax bands

Once you're above the tax-free threshold, tax applies progressively — meaning you're not taxed at one flat rate on your whole income, only on the portion that falls into each band:

  • ₦0 – ₦800,000: 0%
  • ₦800,001 – ₦3,000,000: 15%
  • ₦3,000,001 – ₦12,000,000: 18%
  • ₦12,000,001 – ₦25,000,000: 21%
  • ₦25,000,001 – ₦50,000,000: 23%
  • Above ₦50,000,000: 25%
This trips a lot of people up: earning ₦4,000,000 in taxable income doesn't mean you're taxed at 18% on the whole amount. The first ₦800,000 is tax-free, the next ₦2,200,000 is taxed at 15%, and only the remaining ₦1,000,000 is taxed at 18%.

What else changed: rent relief replaces CRA

The old Consolidated Relief Allowance — 21% of gross income, subtracted before tax bands applied — no longer exists. In its place, the new law introduces a rent relief: 20% of your annual rent paid, capped at ₦500,000 per year. If you pay rent and can document it (a lease agreement and receipts, which your employer may ask for), this reduces your taxable income. If your rent is high, the ₦500,000 cap applies regardless — you don't get more relief just because you pay more rent.

For some people this new structure works out better than the old CRA; for others it's less generous — it depends heavily on your specific salary and rent numbers, which is exactly why a rough online guess isn't reliable and an actual calculation matters.

Pension and NHF: what's still the same, what's changed

  • Pension stays at 8% of basic salary + housing allowance + transport allowance (not your full gross pay) — this is unchanged from before.
  • NHF (National Housing Fund) at 2.5% is now voluntary for private-sector employees under the new law, though it remains mandatory for public-sector workers. If you're private-sector, check with your employer on how this is being handled.

A worked example

Take someone earning ₦500,000 a month gross (₦6,000,000 a year), split as ₦250,000 basic, ₦100,000 housing, ₦100,000 transport, and ₦50,000 other allowances, paying ₦1,800,000 a year in rent:

  • Pension: 8% × (₦250,000 + ₦100,000 + ₦100,000) × 12 = ₦432,000
  • NHF (if opted in): 2.5% × ₦250,000 × 12 = ₦75,000
  • Rent relief: 20% × ₦1,800,000 = ₦360,000 (under the ₦500,000 cap, so the full amount applies)
  • Taxable income: ₦6,000,000 − ₦432,000 − ₦75,000 − ₦360,000 = ₦5,133,000
  • PAYE: ₦0 (first ₦800,000) + 15% × ₦2,200,000 (₦330,000) + 18% × ₦2,133,000 (₦383,940) = ₦713,940 a year, or roughly ₦59,495 a month

Net effect: on a ₦500,000 monthly gross salary, take-home lands around ₦398,255 after pension, NHF, and PAYE — the exact figure depends on your specific salary structure and rent. Why "just Google it" gives you the wrong number

A lot of online calculators haven't been updated, or quietly mix 2025 and 2026 rules — old CRA with new bands, or new bands with the old NHF treatment. Given how different the two systems are, a calculator that isn't explicitly built for the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 will give you a number that's confidently wrong, not just slightly off.

Frequently asked questions

What is the tax-free threshold in Nigeria for 2026?
The first ₦800,000 of annual taxable income is tax-free under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025, effective from 1 January 2026 — roughly ₦66,667 a month.

Is NHF compulsory in Nigeria in 2026?
For private-sector employees, NHF contributions (2.5%) are now voluntary under the new law. Public-sector employees remain mandatory contributors.

What replaced the Consolidated Relief Allowance (CRA)?
CRA has been abolished. In its place, employees can claim rent relief — 20% of their annual rent paid, capped at ₦500,000 a year — provided they can show documentation like a lease agreement and rent receipts.

Do I pay more or less tax under the new 2026 bands?
It depends on your income level. Lower earners generally pay significantly less due to the higher tax-free threshold, while very high earners face a slightly higher top rate (25%) than before — the exact effect depends on your specific salary and rent.

Is pension still calculated the same way in 2026?
Yes — pension remains 8% of basic salary plus housing and transport allowances, unchanged from the previous system.
