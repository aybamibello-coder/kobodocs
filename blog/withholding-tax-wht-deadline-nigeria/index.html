Withholding Tax (WHT) Deadline Nigeria: How to Track Deductions and Avoid Penalties

August 2026 · 6 min read

If a client has ever paid you a little less than your invoice said, with a note like "WHT deducted," you've already met withholding tax. What most small business owners don't realise is that the moment doesn't end there — that deduction comes with a 21-day clock attached, and if the client (or you, when you're the one deducting) misses it, FIRS doesn't care whose fault the delay was.

Key takeaways
  • WHT remittance is due within 21 days of the deduction — not 21 days from the invoice date.
  • You should receive a WHT credit note or certificate for every deduction — without it, you can't offset the amount against your own tax later.
  • If your business also pays contractors or vendors, you're the one responsible for deducting and remitting on time.
  • Tracking WHT on a spreadsheet works until you have more than a handful of clients — then deadlines start slipping silently.

Why the 21-day deadline catches people off guard

Most Nigerian business owners think about tax deadlines in monthly or annual terms — VAT filing, PAYE, annual returns. WHT doesn't work that way. Each individual deduction starts its own 21-day countdown from the date it was withheld, not from when the invoice was raised or when the financial month closes. If you're dealing with several clients paying on different schedules, you can easily end up with five or six separate WHT clocks running at once, each expiring on a different date.

Two sides of the same obligation

WHT cuts both ways, and it's worth being clear on which side you're on for any given transaction:

  • As the one being paid — your client deducts WHT (commonly 5% or 10%, depending on the service) before paying you, and should give you a credit note or certificate as proof. You'll need that document later to offset the deduction against your own Company Income Tax.
  • As the one paying a vendor or contractor — you're the one responsible for deducting and remitting within the 21-day window, and for issuing the certificate. Missing this exposes your business to the penalty, not your vendor.
The certificate matters as much as the deadline. A WHT deduction without a corresponding certificate is money you can't easily claim back against your own tax bill — chase the paperwork at the time, not months later when the client's accountant has moved on.

What actually goes wrong

In practice, it's rarely one big missed deadline — it's several small ones. A business invoices five clients in a month, each deducts WHT on a different date, and by the time anyone checks, two or three remittances are already overdue. Because each deduction is small relative to the business's overall tax picture, it's easy for this to go unnoticed until a certificate is missing at filing time, or a penalty notice arrives for a remittance nobody tracked.

How to actually stay on top of it

The fix isn't complicated in principle — log every deduction the moment it happens, note the 21-day deadline, and don't let a certificate go unrequested. The part that's hard is doing this consistently by hand across every client and every month. A running log (spreadsheet or otherwise) that flags upcoming deadlines before they're missed is the difference between this being routine and being a recurring source of penalties.
