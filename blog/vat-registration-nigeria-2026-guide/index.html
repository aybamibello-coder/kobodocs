VAT Registration for Small Businesses in Nigeria (2026): What Changed, What Didn't

August 2026 · 6 min read

If you've heard that the small business tax threshold in Nigeria jumped to ₦100 million in 2026 and assumed that also means you don't need to worry about VAT until you're much bigger — that's the single most common misreading of the new tax law, and it can leave a business charging clients without a VAT number it should already have, or worse, not charging VAT it was legally required to collect. The ₦100 million figure and the VAT threshold are two different numbers, for two different taxes. Here's the actual difference.

Key takeaways
  • The VAT registration threshold is still ₦25 million in annual turnover — the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 did not change it.
  • What did change: the small company exemption from Companies Income Tax rose from ₦25 million to ₦100 million turnover (with fixed assets under ₦250 million).
  • These are separate thresholds for separate taxes. A business can owe zero Companies Income Tax and still be legally required to register for and charge VAT.
  • The VAT rate itself is unchanged at 7.5%.
  • Structured e-invoicing is being phased in for VAT-registered businesses through 2026, after starting with large taxpayers in late 2025.

The threshold that changed, and the one that didn't

On 1 January 2026, four new tax laws took effect together — the Nigeria Tax Act 2025, the Nigeria Tax Administration Act 2025, the Nigeria Revenue Service (Establishment) Act 2025, and the Joint Revenue Board (Establishment) Act 2025. Between them, they replaced the old VAT Act framework and renamed the Federal Inland Revenue Service the Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS). A lot changed. The VAT registration threshold wasn't one of those things.

What actually moved is the small company exemption from Companies Income Tax: previously set at ₦25 million turnover, it's now ₦100 million turnover with total fixed assets under ₦250 million — a genuinely large relief for small businesses on income tax. Because that number is so widely reported, and because it used to match the VAT threshold almost exactly (₦25 million), it's easy to assume both moved together. They didn't.

Two thresholds, two different taxes

TaxThreshold2026 status
VAT registration₦25 million annual turnoverUnchanged
Companies Income Tax (small company exemption)₦100 million turnover, <₦250 million fixed assetsRaised from ₦25 million

The practical trap: a business with, say, ₦40 million annual turnover now qualifies as a "small company" for income tax purposes and pays 0% CIT — genuinely good news. But that same business is well above the ₦25 million VAT threshold, meaning it should already be registered for VAT, charging 7.5% on taxable supplies, and remitting it. Being exempt from one tax says nothing about the other.

A business earning ₦25 million or more in a rolling 12-month period is required to register for VAT — this applies regardless of company size, sector, or whether it also qualifies as a "small company" for income tax purposes.

Do you actually need to register?

  • Mandatory: if your taxable turnover reaches ₦25 million in any 12-month period, VAT registration isn't optional — it applies to resident companies, partnerships, and sole proprietors making taxable supplies in Nigeria.
  • Voluntary: registration is allowed below the threshold, and it's often the right call if most of your clients are VAT-registered corporates who expect a proper VAT invoice to process payment, or if you want to reclaim input VAT on your own purchases.
  • The real-world cost of skipping it: the common failure mode isn't a business deliberately avoiding VAT — it's a business genuinely not realizing it crossed ₦25 million, then losing weeks to registering under pressure once a corporate client's procurement team asks for a VAT number the business doesn't have.

What e-invoicing means for you

Structured e-invoicing — generating, validating, and transmitting invoices through the national system rather than just issuing a PDF or paper invoice — started with large taxpayers in late 2025 and is being extended to medium and small VAT-registered businesses through 2026. If you're VAT-registered, treat this as something to plan for rather than a distant formality: the direction is toward every VAT invoice being digitally structured and traceable, not just handed to a client as a document.

Keeping invoices VAT-compliant either way

Whether you're already registered or getting ready to be, every invoice you issue needs the VAT rate and amount shown clearly, alongside your VAT registration number once you have one. Frequently asked questions

Did the VAT registration threshold change in 2026?
No — the VAT registration threshold remains ₦25 million in annual turnover, unchanged by the Nigeria Tax Act 2025. What changed is a different, separate threshold: the small company exemption from Companies Income Tax, which rose from ₦25 million to ₦100 million.

What's the difference between the VAT threshold and the small company CIT exemption?
They're two different taxes with two different thresholds. VAT registration is required above ₦25 million turnover. Companies Income Tax exemption (0% CIT) applies to companies with turnover up to ₦100 million and fixed assets up to ₦250 million. A business can be exempt from CIT and still be required to register for and charge VAT.

Can a business under ₦25 million voluntarily register for VAT?
Yes. Voluntary registration is allowed below the threshold, and can be worthwhile if most of your clients are VAT-registered corporates who expect a VAT invoice, or if you want to reclaim input VAT on your own business purchases.

What is the current VAT rate in Nigeria?
7.5%, applied to taxable goods and services. This rate hasn't changed under the 2026 reforms.

Is e-invoicing mandatory for small businesses now?
The e-invoicing rollout is phased. Large taxpayers were brought in first, in late 2025. Medium and small VAT-registered businesses are being brought into mandatory structured e-invoicing from 2026 as the rollout continues.
