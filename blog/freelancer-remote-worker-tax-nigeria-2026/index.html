Nigeria's 2026 Tax Act: What Freelancers & Remote Workers Must Know

August 2026 · 7 min read

A growing number of Nigerians earn mainly from clients and employers abroad — designers, developers, writers, virtual assistants, consultants, paid in USD, GBP or EUR through Upwork, Fiverr, direct contracts, or a remote job. Until this year, that income mostly stayed off the radar. The Nigeria Tax Act 2025, effective January 2026, changes that for good.

Key takeaways
  • Nigerian tax residents are now taxed on worldwide income — including foreign-currency earnings from clients or employers abroad.
  • You're exempt if your total annual income, after deductions, is ₦800,000 or less.
  • Nigerian clients can withhold tax on your behalf; foreign clients can't, so it's on you to register, keep records, and self-assess.
  • Penalties are real: up to ₦25,000/month for late registration, ₦50,000/month for late filing, and up to ₦1,000,000 or imprisonment for false declarations.
  • Annual returns are generally due by March 31, filed via the TaxPro Max portal.

Who this affects

If you live in Nigeria and are a Nigerian tax resident, your worldwide income is now in scope — regardless of where the client, employer, or platform paying you is based. This covers two overlapping groups: remote employees on a foreign company's payroll (treated similarly to any salaried worker, just without local PAYE deduction), and freelancers/contractors invoicing multiple clients directly, who are treated as running a small business and are responsible for their own records and filing.

If you live and work outside Nigeria and aren't a Nigerian tax resident, foreign-sourced income generally isn't taxed here — this law is about residence, not nationality.

What you actually owe

The first ₦800,000 of annual taxable income, after allowable deductions, is tax-free. Above that, tax applies progressively — you don't pay the higher band's rate on your entire income, only on the portion that falls within it. Foreign-currency income is converted to naira at the exchange rate on the date you received it, not the date you invoiced.

A client deducting withholding tax (WHT) before paying you isn't your final tax bill — it's an advance credit. When you file your annual return, you calculate your full liability and offset what's already been withheld. Foreign clients generally can't withhold Nigerian tax at source, so that portion is entirely on you to self-assess.

What happens if you don't comply

Miss thisPenalty
Registering with the tax authority₦50,000 first month, then ₦25,000 every month after
Filing your annual return₦100,000 first month, then ₦50,000 every month after
False declarationsUp to ₦1,000,000, or up to 3 years imprisonment, or both

Most foreign inflows are already linked to Nigerian bank accounts and BVNs, and international tax-information agreements mean this is increasingly visible to the tax authority — this isn't a rule that relies on nobody noticing.

What to actually track

Whatever your final liability turns out to be, the paper trail matters as much as the number. Keep, and hold onto for several years:

  • Every invoice you send, with the currency clearly stated
  • Client contracts or agreements
  • Bank, Payoneer, or Wise statements showing what actually landed and when
  • Proof of any withholding tax a Nigerian client already deducted

This is the exact gap most freelancers have today — invoices sent over WhatsApp with no consistent record, income scattered across Payoneer, Wise, and a local bank account, no single place showing the full year. What you can likely deduct

Reducing your taxable income legitimately starts with knowing what counts as a business expense. Commonly cited deductions for freelancers and remote workers include:

  • Up to 20% of annual rent, capped at ₦500,000
  • Internet, data, and electricity/fuel costs tied to work
  • Software subscriptions and tools used for the job
  • Pension contributions (8%) and NHF (2.5%, where applicable)
  • Life insurance premiums, up to ₦100,000

The general test: if it's a legitimate cost of doing the work and you can show it, it's worth discussing with an accountant as a deduction.
