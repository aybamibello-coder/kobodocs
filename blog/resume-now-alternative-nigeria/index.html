Resume Now Alternative for Nigeria: A CV Builder Without the Subscription Trap

September 2026 · 6 min read

Resume Now is a well-known, genuinely capable AI resume builder — professional templates, guided writing prompts, a large user base. For Nigerian job seekers specifically, though, its business model creates a predictable problem: it runs on a short trial that auto-renews into a recurring USD subscription unless you cancel in time, which is a real source of frustration recruiters and reviewers alike have flagged for exactly this reason.

Key takeaways
  • Resume Now's trial auto-renews into a recurring USD subscription unless cancelled — a routine complaint in its own reviews, and a bigger issue for Nigerian cards than for US ones.
  • Most job seekers only need a CV builder for a few weeks at a time, not an ongoing monthly subscription.
  • A Naira-priced, pay-per-download model avoids the auto-renewal risk entirely — you pay once for the CV you need, not a recurring charge you have to remember to cancel.
  • Template quality and ease of use are comparable for the core job of building a clean, professional one-or-two-page CV.

What Resume Now does well

To be fair to it: Resume Now's guided, step-by-step builder is genuinely good for someone unsure how to phrase their experience, and its template library is broad. If you're comfortable with a USD subscription and plan to use it repeatedly over months, it's a solid product on its own terms.

Where the model creates friction for Nigerian users

  • USD billing on a Nigerian card. International card charges, currency conversion, and occasional bank declines add friction that a Naira-billed alternative avoids entirely.
  • The auto-renewal structure. A short trial that rolls into a recurring monthly charge is a common complaint pattern across resume-builder reviews generally — the kind of subscription you have to actively remember to cancel, or get billed again for a CV you already downloaded weeks ago.
  • Mismatch with actual usage. Most people need a CV builder in short, intense bursts — before a specific application round, before NYSC ends, right after graduation — not as a standing monthly tool.

What a pay-per-download model looks like instead

KoboDocs Resume flips the structure: building, editing, and previewing your CV is free indefinitely, with no login and no trial clock running. When you're ready to download a clean copy, you pay once — ₦1,000 per clean download — rather than starting a subscription you need to remember to cancel. There's no auto-renewal to track, because there's nothing recurring to begin with.

If you're actively job-hunting for months, not weeks: KoboDocs Pro (₦1,500/mo) includes unlimited clean downloads across every KoboDocs tool, not just the CV builder — worth it if you're updating your CV repeatedly across a long search, but entirely optional either way.

Template and feature comparison

For the core job — a clean, ATS-readable one or two-page CV — both platforms cover the basics: contact header, summary, work experience with bullet points, education, and skills. Resume Now has a deeper template library and more AI-assisted phrasing prompts; KoboDocs Resume keeps the template count smaller (Classic and Modern) but covers the layouts most Nigerian recruiters expect without extra frills to navigate.

How to decide

If you want the widest template selection and don't mind managing a USD subscription (and remembering to cancel it), Resume Now is a capable choice. If you'd rather avoid a recurring charge altogether and only pay when you actually download a finished CV, a Naira-priced, pay-per-download builder removes that risk from the equation entirely.
