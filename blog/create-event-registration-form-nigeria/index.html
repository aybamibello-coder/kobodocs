How to Create an Event, Church, or School Registration Form Online — Free

September 2026 · 5 min read

Whether it's a wedding RSVP, a church camp registration, or a school's new-admission intake, the underlying problem is the same: you need a way to collect names, contact details, and sometimes payment confirmation from a large group of people, without chasing individual WhatsApp messages or losing track of a paper register.

Key takeaways
  • A digital registration form beats a paper register or a WhatsApp broadcast because responses land in one organized place automatically.
  • WhatsApp share links and printable QR codes work better than a plain web link for Nigerian audiences, especially for in-person events.
  • Build in file upload only if you genuinely need it (ID, passport photo) — every extra required field reduces completion rate.
  • Never collect payment directly through the form tool itself; link out to your own payment method instead.

Step 1: Decide what you actually need to collect

Before building anything, list the minimum fields you need — name, phone number, and maybe one or two more. Every additional required field lowers how many people finish the form, so resist the urge to ask for everything "just in case."

Step 2: Start from a template instead of a blank page

Event RSVP, church/mosque registration, and school admission are all common enough that a ready-made template will already have the right field structure — you're editing labels and adding your organization's branding rather than designing the form logic from scratch.

Step 3: Add conditional logic if your registration branches

If your event has different tracks (adults vs. children, VIP vs. general, boarding vs. day student), conditional logic lets you show only the relevant follow-up questions based on an earlier answer — instead of one long form with irrelevant sections for every respondent.

Step 4: Decide if you need file uploads

For school admissions or NYSC documentation, you may genuinely need a passport photograph or ID copy uploaded directly. For a general event RSVP, skip this — it adds friction and most respondents will fill it in from a phone where locating and uploading a file is more effort than typing an answer.

Step 5: Share it the way people will actually open it

  • WhatsApp share link — for event or church groups where WhatsApp is the primary communication channel.
  • Printable QR code — for physical event entrances, church notice boards, or school reception desks, so people can scan and register on the spot.
  • Plain web link — for email, social media captions, or a website.
On payment: If your event has a registration fee, don't expect the form itself to process it. Add a field with your Paystack link, bank transfer details, or a "proof of payment" upload instead — the form should collect information, not hold money.

Step 6: Check your responses come in as they arrive

A live response count matters most in the final days before an event or deadline — you want to know if registration is trailing so you can push a reminder, not find out on the day itself that only a fraction of expected attendees actually signed up.

Frequently asked questions

How many registrations can I collect on a free plan?
KoboDocs Forms' free tier covers up to 100 submissions a month across 3 forms — enough for most single events or a small school's admission window.

Can I close registration once I hit a capacity limit?
Yes — you can take the form link down or disable it once you've reached your target number, since responses aren't tied to a fixed schedule.

Is a QR code registration form secure enough for an official school or church record?
The form itself is as secure as any web form with encrypted storage; whether it satisfies your institution's specific record-keeping policy is worth checking against their own requirements first.
