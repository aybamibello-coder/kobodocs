How to Register a Business Name with CAC in Nigeria: Step-by-Step Guide

July 2026 · 9 min read

Registering with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) is the step that turns "I sell things" into an actual, legally recognized business — the thing that lets you open a business bank account, apply for grants and loans, and put a real registration number on your invoices instead of just a WhatsApp number.

It's also a process a lot of first-time founders put off simply because it seems more complicated than it is. Here's the actual step-by-step, plus the mistakes that most often get an application rejected or delayed.

Key takeaways
  • Registering a Business Name is faster and cheaper than registering a Limited Liability Company, but a Business Name doesn't create a separate legal entity — you remain personally liable for the business's debts.
  • The process starts with a name availability search and reservation before anything else can happen — don't skip this step or assume your preferred name is free.
  • Most rejections come down to a handful of avoidable issues: a name too similar to an existing one, use of a restricted word, or incomplete proprietor documentation.
  • CAC fees and requirements are revised periodically — always confirm current costs rather than relying on a number you saw somewhere online months ago.
  • Registration is just the first step — a registered company also needs the post-incorporation paperwork (board resolution, share certificates) before a bank will fully treat it as operational.

Business Name vs. Limited Liability Company — which one do you need?

Before anything else, decide which structure fits: a Business Name registration is simpler, cheaper, and faster — suited to sole proprietors and small partnerships who mainly need a registered trading name. A Limited Liability Company (LLC) is a separate legal entity from its owners, meaning your personal assets are generally shielded if the business runs into debt or legal trouble — but it comes with more paperwork, more ongoing compliance (annual returns, statutory registers), and a longer registration process.

If you're a freelancer or a small trader just getting a registered name for invoicing and banking purposes, Business Name registration is usually the right starting point. If you're raising investment, taking on partners with defined shareholding, or want the liability protection, register a Limited Liability Company instead.

Step 1: Search and reserve your business name

Every registration starts with a name availability check through CAC's portal — you propose one or two names, and CAC checks whether anything identical or confusingly similar is already registered. If your first choice is available, it gets reserved for you for a limited window while you complete the rest of the registration. This step alone eliminates most of the "my application got rejected" stories, since a large share of rejections come from picking a name too close to an existing one.

Step 2: Prepare your documents

For a standard Business Name registration, you'll typically need:

  • A valid means of identification (National ID, international passport, or driver's license) for the proprietor(s).
  • A passport photograph.
  • The registered business address.
  • A clear description of the nature of the business (what you actually do, in CAC's classification terms).
  • For partnerships, the same documents for each partner involved.

Step 3: Complete the registration and pay the fees

Once your name is reserved and documents are ready, the registration form is completed with proprietor details, business address, and nature of business, and the applicable CAC fee is paid. Fees and exact requirements are revised by CAC from time to time, so rather than quoting a specific number here that may already be out of date by the time you read this, use KoboDocs' CAC registration cost calculator for a current estimate before you budget for it.

Step 4: Receive your certificate

Once approved, CAC issues a registration certificate with your unique registration number (the "RC" or "BN" number, depending on structure). This is what goes on your invoices, your business bank account application, and any grant or loan application that asks for proof of registration.

Common reasons CAC registrations get rejected or delayed

  • Name too similar to an existing registration — the most common rejection reason by far. Always check availability before getting attached to a specific name.
  • Restricted or reserved words — names implying government affiliation, or using words like "Federal," "National," "Chartered," or similar, generally require special approval and get flagged otherwise.
  • Incomplete or mismatched proprietor ID — the name on your ID should match what's entered on the form; small mismatches cause delays.
  • Vague nature-of-business description — being specific about what the business actually does speeds up processing.
  • Incorrect or incomplete address details — the registered address should be complete and accurate, not a placeholder.

How long does registration take?

Business Name registration is generally the faster of the two options once your documents are complete and your name is approved — often processed within days rather than weeks when there are no issues with the application. Limited Liability Company registration typically takes longer given the additional documentation (Memorandum and Articles of Association, share allocation, and director details). Delays almost always trace back to one of the common issues above rather than the process itself being slow.

What comes after registration

Getting your certificate isn't the finish line, especially for a registered company. Banks and serious business partners will often expect to see the post-incorporation paperwork too — a board resolution authorizing the company to open a bank account, share certificates for the owners, and the statutory registers a company is required to maintain. Frequently asked questions

Can I register a business name by myself, or do I need an accredited agent?
For a straightforward Business Name registration, many people complete it themselves. Limited Liability Company registration in Nigeria generally requires filing through an accredited agent (such as a lawyer or chartered accountant) registered with CAC.

How many business name options should I prepare?
It's worth having at least two options ready — if your first choice is too similar to an existing registration, having a backup avoids restarting the whole search process.

Can I change my business name after registration?
Yes, though it involves a separate process with CAC and is generally more involved than getting the name right the first time — worth avoiding if possible by choosing carefully upfront.

Do I need a business bank account before registering, or after?
After. Banks require your CAC registration certificate (and for companies, the post-incorporation documents) before they'll open a business account — you can't get one first.

Is a Business Name the same as a trademark?
No. CAC registration protects your business name from being registered by someone else with CAC, but it isn't the same as trademark protection, which is a separate registration through the Trademarks Registry for protecting your brand more broadly.
