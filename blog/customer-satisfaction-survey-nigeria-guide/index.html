Customer Satisfaction Survey (CSAT) Guide for Nigerian Businesses

September 2026 · 6 min read

A customer satisfaction survey (CSAT) measures how happy customers are with a specific interaction — a purchase, a support call, a delivery. Done well, it gives you an early warning system for problems before they show up as lost customers. Done poorly, it's a form nobody fills out and a score nobody trusts.

Key takeaways
  • CSAT measures satisfaction with a specific interaction — different from NPS, which measures overall loyalty and willingness to recommend.
  • The standard CSAT question is a simple 1-5 (or 1-3) scale — "How satisfied were you with [specific interaction]?"
  • Send the survey immediately after the interaction, not days later.
  • A CSAT score only matters if you track it consistently over time and act on the low scores.

CSAT vs. NPS — which one do you actually need?

CSAT asks about one specific moment: "How satisfied were you with today's delivery?" It's useful right after a transaction, support interaction, or service visit.

NPS (Net Promoter Score) asks a broader question: "How likely are you to recommend us to a friend?" It measures overall relationship health, not one interaction, and is better run periodically (quarterly, or after a certain number of purchases) rather than after every single transaction.

Most Nigerian small businesses only need CSAT to start — it's simpler to run and directly tied to something you can fix.

Writing a CSAT question that actually works

The most common format is a 1-5 scale tied to a specific, recent interaction:

"How satisfied were you with your delivery today?"
1 (Very dissatisfied) — 5 (Very satisfied)

Avoid vague versions like "How was your experience?" — it's harder to answer and harder to act on, since you won't know which part of the experience the score is actually about.

When to send it

  • Right after checkout or delivery — for retail, e-commerce, or logistics.
  • Right after a support interaction closes — for customer service teams.
  • At the end of a service visit — for salons, repair services, and professional services.

Timing is the single biggest factor in response rate — a survey sent within the hour gets answered; one sent the next day is often ignored.

Calculating your CSAT score: Add up the percentage of respondents who answered 4 or 5 (satisfied or very satisfied). If 80 out of 100 respondents rated 4 or 5, your CSAT score is 80%. Track this number monthly rather than reacting to any single day's results.

What to do with a low score

A CSAT score by itself doesn't fix anything — the value is in the follow-up. Add a short open-text question ("What could we have done better?") to every low rating, and have someone actually read and act on those answers, not just file the number away.

Common mistakes

  • Asking too many questions. CSAT works because it's fast — one rating question plus an optional comment box is usually enough.
  • Sending it too late. A survey sent two days after the interaction gets a fraction of the response rate of one sent within the hour.
  • Never acting on the data. If low scores don't lead to any visible change, customers stop bothering to respond honestly.

Frequently asked questions

What's a good CSAT score?
It varies by industry, but 75-85% is a commonly cited healthy range for many service businesses. What matters more than the absolute number is whether your score is trending up or down over time.

How often should I run a CSAT survey?
After every relevant interaction, not on a fixed schedule — it's designed to measure specific moments, not overall sentiment.

Can I run CSAT and NPS together?
Yes — many businesses run CSAT after every transaction and a separate, less frequent NPS survey (quarterly, for example) to track overall loyalty.
