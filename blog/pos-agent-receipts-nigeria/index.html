Tracking Sales and Issuing Receipts as a POS Agent in Nigeria

August 2026 · 5 min read

POS agency has become one of the most common small businesses in Nigeria — low startup capital, steady demand, and a real path from a modest side income to a full-time earner. The part that gets less attention than the business model itself is the record-keeping: dozens of small transactions a day, each one easy to forget the specifics of by the time evening comes.

Key takeaways
  • High transaction volume with small individual amounts is exactly the pattern that breaks mental tracking.
  • A POS terminal's own transaction history doesn't always cleanly separate commission from the underlying transaction amount.
  • Giving customers a receipt, even a simple one, builds trust and gives both sides a record if a dispute comes up.
  • Logging transactions as they happen beats reconstructing the day from memory at closing time.

Why POS agents lose track more than other small businesses

A shop selling ten products a day can usually reconstruct what happened from memory or a simple notebook — the transactions are distinct and memorable. A POS agent might handle forty or fifty transactions in a day, many of them similar amounts (a ₦5,000 withdrawal, another ₦5,000 withdrawal, a bill payment, a transfer), which makes them genuinely hard to distinguish in memory by the end of the day. The volume itself is what makes tracking difficult, not any particular complexity in each transaction.

The commission tracking problem

A POS terminal's transaction history shows what moved through the machine, but doesn't always make it easy to see, at a glance, how much of that was the agent's actual earning (commission) versus float that passed through. Without a separate running log, it's easy to lose track of what the day actually earned versus what simply passed through the terminal.

Why giving a receipt matters, even for small transactions

It's tempting to skip receipts for small, routine transactions — a ₦2,000 withdrawal doesn't feel like it needs paperwork. But a receipt does two things at once: it gives the customer confidence and a record if there's ever a dispute about whether a transaction happened or for how much, and it gives the agent their own record without relying purely on the terminal's history or memory. For an agent handling dozens of transactions a day, having a receipt trail — even a simple one — makes end-of-day reconciliation far more reliable.

The habit matters more than the tool. Whether it's a paper receipt book, a notebook log, or a digital tool, the actual value comes from doing it consistently for every transaction — not from having a sophisticated system used inconsistently. A simple habit followed every time beats a complex system followed half the time.

A simple daily system that works

Log each transaction as it happens — amount, type, and a running total — rather than trying to reconstruct the day from the terminal's history at closing. At the end of the day, the running total should match (roughly) what the terminal and physical cash on hand show. Any mismatch is easier to catch and investigate the same day than a week later when the details have faded.

Frequently asked questions

Is a receipt legally required for every POS transaction?
It's not universally mandated for every small cash transaction, but it's good practice regardless — it protects both the agent and the customer if a question comes up later.

How is this different from just checking the POS terminal's printed slip?
A terminal slip confirms the transaction went through, but a running log kept separately gives the agent their own day-level view — total transactions, total commission, and a way to spot a discrepancy without digging through terminal history transaction by transaction.

Does this apply to other similar cash-heavy small businesses too?
Yes — the same logic applies to any business with high transaction volume and small individual amounts: mobile money agents, small retail, market stalls. The tracking problem is the same regardless of the specific business.
