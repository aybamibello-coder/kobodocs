CAC Annual Returns in Nigeria (2026): Deadlines, Penalties, and How to Stay Compliant

August 2026 · 6 min read

Registering a business with CAC feels like the finish line — certificate in hand, you move on to actually running the thing. But CAC registration isn't a one-time event; it comes with a yearly obligation that quietly slips off most owners' radar until a status check or a bank due-diligence request turns up an "Inactive" company. Annual returns are that obligation, and missing them is one of the most common, most avoidable compliance mistakes small business owners make in Nigeria.

Key takeaways
  • Annual returns are a yearly statutory update to CAC confirming your company is still active — not a tax payment, and not optional.
  • For private limited companies, filing is due within 42 days of your Annual General Meeting; for a December year-end, that typically means filing by around 30 June the following year.
  • A company's first annual return is due within 18 months of incorporation, even if the business hasn't started trading yet.
  • Per CAC's own fee schedule, late filing costs ₦3,000 per year overdue for a small company, or ₦5,000 per year for other private companies — and it only gets more expensive the longer it's left.
  • Missed filings change your company's status to Inactive and can block other CAC services until cleared, with struck-off status the eventual consequence of prolonged default.

What an annual return actually is

An annual return is a statutory report filed with the Corporate Affairs Commission once a year, confirming that your company or business name is still active, that its registered details (directors, shareholders, address) are current, and that it's being run in line with the Companies and Allied Matters Act (CAMA) 2020. It is not a tax filing — it doesn't touch FIRS or the Nigeria Revenue Service, and it doesn't calculate anything you owe. It's closer to a yearly "we're still here, here's our current information" confirmation. That doesn't make it any less mandatory.

The actual deadline

  • Private limited companies: due within 42 days of your Annual General Meeting (AGM). For most companies with a 31 December financial year-end, that works out to a filing deadline around 30 June the following year.
  • First return after incorporation: due within 18 months of the company being registered — this applies even if the business hasn't commenced trading yet. Non-operation isn't an exemption.
  • Business names: filed once per calendar year, on a schedule distinct from limited companies — check your specific registration date against the current CAC portal requirement.

What it actually costs to be late

CAC's own published penalty schedule sets late filing of an annual return for a small company at ₦3,000 per year overdue, and ₦5,000 per year for a private company that doesn't qualify as small. Those figures sound modest in isolation — the real cost shows up when a filing has been missed for several years in a row, since the penalty applies per year of default, not as a single flat fee. A business that's gone three years without filing isn't looking at one penalty; it's looking at three, plus the original filing fees for each of those years.

Penalty amounts are set by CAC and can change — always confirm the current figure on the CAC portal before filing, rather than relying on a number from an old article (including this one).

Beyond the fee: what "Inactive" actually blocks

The financial penalty is only part of the cost. Once a company falls behind on annual returns, its status on the CAC register changes from Active to Inactive — and CAC will generally require outstanding returns to be cleared before processing other requests: changing directors, updating a registered address, obtaining a certified true copy, or increasing share capital. If a bank, investor, or corporate client runs a status check during due diligence and finds an Inactive company, that's a credibility problem showing up at exactly the wrong moment. Left unresolved for long enough, continued default can lead to the company being struck off the register entirely, which is a considerably more expensive problem to reverse than paying a late filing fee on time would have been.

Staying ahead of it

The single most effective fix is the least exciting one: know your specific deadline and don't rely on remembering it a year from now. A business's AGM date, incorporation date, and filing history are specific to that business — a generic "file by June" reminder isn't precise enough once you're tracking VAT, WHT, CAC, and other obligations at the same time. Frequently asked questions

When are CAC annual returns due in Nigeria?
For private limited companies, within 42 days of your Annual General Meeting — for a company with a December year-end, that typically works out to filing by around 30 June the following year. A company's first annual return is due within 18 months of incorporation. Business names file once per calendar year on a different schedule.

What happens if I miss the CAC annual returns deadline?
Your company's status on the CAC portal changes from Active to Inactive, a late filing penalty applies per year overdue, and continued non-compliance can eventually lead to the company being struck off the register entirely. An inactive or struck-off status also blocks other CAC services, like changing directors or getting a certified true copy, until outstanding returns are cleared.

How much is the penalty for late CAC annual returns?
Per CAC's own published fee schedule, late filing for a small company's annual return is ₦3,000 per year overdue; for a private company that isn't classed as small, it's ₦5,000 per year overdue. Fees are subject to change, so confirm the current figure on the CAC portal before filing.

Is filing annual returns the same as paying tax?
No. CAC annual returns are a statutory update confirming your company is still active and that its registered details are current — they're not a tax payment. Filing annual tax returns with the tax authority is a separate, additional obligation.

Do dormant or non-operating companies still need to file annual returns?
Yes. The obligation to file applies regardless of whether the company has commenced business activity — as long as it's registered with CAC, it needs to file.
