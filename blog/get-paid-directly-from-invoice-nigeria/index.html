How to Get Paid Directly From Your Invoice in Nigeria

August 2026 · 7 min read

You send the invoice. Then you send a second message with your account number. Then the client sends it again a week later because they lost the first one, or types the account number wrong and the transfer bounces, or pays and forgets to say which invoice it was for. None of this is really about the client being difficult — it's that a PDF invoice on its own doesn't actually tell anyone how to pay it. Here's how to fix that, without signing up for a payment gateway or letting anyone else touch your money.

Key takeaways
  • Most late and mismatched payments in Nigeria aren't about unwilling clients — they're about bank transfer being reference-less by default, so payments arrive with no way to tell which invoice they're for.
  • Putting your bank details directly on the invoice (not in a separate message) removes the single biggest point of friction.
  • A QR code that encodes your account details lets a client scan instead of retype — this cuts the #1 cause of failed transfers: a mistyped account number.
  • If you already have a Paystack, Flutterwave, Opay, or Moniepoint link, adding it to your invoice gives clients a one-click option too.
  • None of this makes KoboDocs a payment processor — money still moves straight between your client and your own account or provider.

Why a separate WhatsApp message is the actual problem

Bank transfer is how the overwhelming majority of Nigerian business-to-business payments happen. That's not the issue. The issue is that a standard transfer has no required reference field tying it back to a specific invoice, and most senders don't fill one in even when there's space for it. So when a payment lands in your account, you're often looking at a name and an amount with no way to match it to an outstanding invoice without asking around. Multiply that by every client who pays a slightly different amount, on a slightly different day, from a slightly different account name than what's on file, and reconciliation turns into an actual weekly chore.

Sending your account details in a separate WhatsApp message doesn't fix any of this — it just adds a second thing that can go missing, get retyped wrong, or arrive after the client already forgot they owe you. The details need to live on the same document as the amount and the invoice number, not in a message next to it.

The three things that actually make an invoice payable

  1. Clear bank details, on the invoice itself. Bank name, account number, account name — not squeezed into a footer next to your phone number, but laid out as its own section so it's the second thing a client sees after the total.
  2. A QR code your client can scan instead of retype. Most failed or delayed transfers in Nigeria come down to one wrong digit in a 10-digit account number, typed from a screenshot or a WhatsApp message. A QR code that encodes those same details removes the retyping step entirely — scan it, and the details are right there to copy, no transcription involved.
  3. Your own payment link, if you already have one. If you've got a Paystack, Flutterwave, Opay, or Moniepoint link from your own account, put it on the invoice too. Some clients will always prefer clicking a link over opening their banking app, and there's no reason to make them ask you for it separately.
A quick but important distinction: a QR code that encodes your bank details is not the same thing as Nigeria's official NQR payment system, which is a CBN-regulated, bank-certified standard. What this gets you is accuracy — your client scans instead of retyping a 10-digit account number by hand — not a certified instant-payment protocol. It's a details shortcut, and it's honestly labelled as one.

Frequently asked questions

Can I really get paid directly from an invoice without a payment gateway?
Yes, in the sense that matters most in Nigeria: your bank details, a scannable QR code, and your own payment link (if you have one) all appear on the invoice itself, so the client doesn't need a separate message from you to know how to pay. The money still moves the normal way — bank transfer to your account, or through whichever provider your payment link belongs to.

Is the QR code the same as Nigeria's official NQR payment system?
No, and it's worth being precise about this. Nigeria's NQR is a CBN-regulated, bank-certified standard for QR payments. A QR code on your KoboDocs invoice is a plain, scannable version of your bank details — it saves your client from retyping your account number by hand, which is where most transfer errors happen, but it's not a certified instant-payment protocol. Treat it as an accurate-details shortcut, not a payment network.

Does KoboDocs process or hold the payment?
No. KoboDocs is not a financial institution and never touches, holds, or moves your money. Bank transfers go straight into your own account. If you add a payment link, that link belongs to your own Paystack, Flutterwave, Opay, or Moniepoint account — KoboDocs only displays it.

What if I don't already have a payment link?
You don't need one. Bank details plus the QR code work on their own — most Nigerian B2B payments are bank transfers anyway. The payment link field is there for businesses that already have a Paystack, Flutterwave, Opay, or Moniepoint link and want it on their invoices too; it's optional.

Is this free?
Yes. Adding your bank details and payment link, and having them appear automatically on invoices, is part of Business Suite — there's no extra charge for this specific feature.
