How to Convert Word to PDF

August 2026 · 5 min read

"Please submit your CV as a PDF, not a Word document" shows up on job listings constantly, and for good reason — a .docx file can look completely different depending on what device or software opens it, while a PDF looks exactly the same everywhere. If you drafted your CV in Word (which most people do), converting it to PDF before sending is a small step that avoids a real problem.

Key takeaways
  • A PDF displays identically on every device — a Word document can shift fonts, spacing, and layout depending on what opens it.
  • Most employers, job portals, and application forms explicitly prefer or require PDF for exactly this reason.
  • Converting Word to PDF preserves your original formatting — headings, bullet points, and paragraph structure carry over.
  • Complex elements like tables, embedded images, or unusual formatting are worth double-checking after conversion, since results can vary with more complex documents.
  • A PDF also can't be easily edited by the recipient — useful when you want your CV or proposal to arrive exactly as you sent it.

Why PDF over Word specifically

A Word document's appearance depends on the software and fonts installed on whoever's opening it — a CV that looks perfectly formatted on your laptop can shift line breaks, change fonts, or misalign spacing on someone else's device, especially across different Word versions or when opened in software other than Microsoft Word. A PDF locks the layout in place — what you see when you export it is exactly what the recipient sees, regardless of their device or software.

Why job portals and employers ask for it

Beyond consistent display, PDF is harder to accidentally (or intentionally) edit, which matters for anything meant to arrive exactly as sent — a CV, a signed cover letter, a formal proposal. Many application tracking systems and online portals are also built to parse PDF more reliably than Word documents, which is part of why so many job listings specify it directly.

What carries over well in conversion

  • Headings and text formatting (bold, italic, font choices)
  • Bullet points and numbered lists
  • Paragraph structure and spacing
  • Basic page layout

What's worth double-checking

Documents with tables, embedded images, or unusual custom formatting can convert less predictably than a straightforward text document — it's worth opening the resulting PDF and checking it looks right before sending anything important, particularly if your CV or report includes a table, a photo, or non-standard layout elements.

Frequently asked questions

Why do employers ask for a CV in PDF instead of Word?
A PDF displays identically on every device, while a Word document's fonts, spacing, and layout can shift depending on what software or device opens it — employers want to see the CV exactly as you formatted it.

Does converting Word to PDF change the formatting?
Standard elements like headings, bullet points, and paragraph structure typically carry over well. Documents with tables, embedded images, or unusual formatting are worth double-checking after conversion, since results can vary.

Can someone easily edit a PDF I send them?
Not as easily as a Word document — this is one reason PDF is preferred for anything meant to arrive exactly as sent, like a CV, signed letter, or formal proposal.

Do I need Microsoft Word installed to convert a document to PDF?
No — a browser-based converter can turn a .docx file into a PDF without needing Word or any other software installed.

Why do job application portals often require PDF specifically?
Many application tracking systems are built to parse PDF more reliably than Word documents, and PDF's consistent display across devices reduces formatting issues on the reviewer's end.
