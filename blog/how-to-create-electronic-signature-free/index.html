How to Create an Electronic Signature (Free, No Software Needed)

September 2026 · 5 min read

An electronic signature is just a way of showing "this person agreed to this," in a form a computer can capture and keep a record of. There's no special software you need to own, and no certificate to buy. There are three common ways to create one — which one you should use depends on what the document is actually for.

Key takeaways
  • You can create an electronic signature by drawing it, typing it in a signature-style font, or uploading a photo of your handwritten one — all free.
  • For anything informal (internal approvals, personal notes), any of the three is fine.
  • For anything that matters legally — a contract, an offer letter, an agreement — the signature itself matters less than whether the platform keeps an audit trail of who signed and when.
  • An electronic signature is legally valid in Nigeria under the Evidence Act and CAMA, provided that audit trail exists — see the full legal guide.

Method 1: Draw it

The most common method. Using your finger on a phone or tablet, or a mouse/trackpad on a computer, you draw your signature directly on screen the way you would on paper. Most e-signature tools, KoboDocs included, offer a signature pad for exactly this — open KoboDocs e-Signature, and there's a blank canvas to draw on before placing it on your document.

Best for: anything where the signature needs to look like an actual signature — most contracts, agreements, and official documents.

Method 2: Type it

You type your full name, and the tool renders it in a cursive or signature-style font. This is faster than drawing but looks visibly "typed" rather than handwritten — some people find this less appropriate for formal documents, though it carries the same legal weight as a drawn signature when the platform records who typed it and when.

Best for: quick internal approvals, acknowledgments, and documents where speed matters more than appearance.

Method 3: Upload a photo of your handwritten signature

Sign a blank piece of paper, take a photo or scan it, and upload the image. Most tools will let you crop it and remove the white background so it sits cleanly on the document. This is the closest to a "real" signature in appearance, at the cost of an extra step.

Best for: anyone who wants their actual handwritten signature to appear consistently across every document they sign electronically, rather than redrawing it each time.

What actually matters isn't which method you pick

All three methods are legally equivalent — Nigerian law doesn't require a specific signature style, and none of them are inherently "more official" than the others. What actually determines whether a signature holds up if a document is ever disputed is whether there's a record of who signed, when, and from what device — the audit trail, not the signature's appearance. A beautifully drawn signature with no audit trail is weaker evidence than a typed one that's properly logged.

This is the difference between a basic PDF signature tool and a proper e-signature platform. Drawing your signature in any free PDF editor works fine for signing your own document. If you're sending a document to someone else and it needs to hold up later, you need the audit trail too — covered in our step-by-step guide to signing and sending PDFs.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to use the same signature every time?
No — you can draw, type, or upload a different signature each time you sign something. Some people prefer consistency (usually via the upload method) so it looks the same across documents, but it isn't a legal requirement.

Is a typed signature as legally valid as a drawn one?
Yes — what matters legally is the audit trail behind it (who signed, when), not whether the signature was drawn, typed, or uploaded.

Can I create an electronic signature without an account?
Yes, for signing your own document — KoboDocs e-Signature lets you draw or type a signature and download the signed file without creating an account. An account is only needed if you're the one sending documents out to others.

Is this different from a "digital signature"?
Yes, technically — a digital signature specifically refers to a cryptographic method using a certificate, while "electronic signature" is the broader legal term covering drawn, typed, and uploaded signatures alike. In everyday use (and in this guide), "electronic signature" is what almost everyone means and needs.
